## PDF page 8 (printed 6): 1.1 Main functions of the KDV 4 supply module

**Provision of drive power.** The controlled three-phase bridge of the KDV 4 rectifies the mains a.c. voltage. The KDV 4 operates as an inverter when the drives are in the generator mode. A storage capacitor provides the necessary current peaks during rapid load changes. In the event of a mains failure or power disconnection the energy fed back by the drives is absorbed by a bleeder resistor connected in parallel with the storage capacitor.

**Supply to electronics circuits.** The KDV 4 provides the +24 VL and the ±15 VM for all connected drive modules. In the event of a mains failure the electronics are supplied from the link circuit d.c. voltage. Consequently the drive electronics are still operational when the drives are in the generator mode.

**Drive system monitoring.** The KDV 4 has extensive monitoring functions. These communicate with the drive modules via the signal voltage bus. The **Bb1 contact** of the KDV 4 is very important for the drive system. The power supply can only be connected when it is closed.

## PDF page 9 (printed 7): 2. Applications

The KDV 4 with regen. power capability is an economic solution for continuous drive power ratings from about 10 kW and continuous regenerated power levels above 1 kW. When the KDV 4 is used, no power transformer is required for mains voltages of 3 x 380 to 460 V a.c., 50/60 Hz. The KDV 2 supply module with built-in 2 kW bleeder can be used for lower regen. power levels.

**Fig. 2: Power range when using the KDV 4 supply module** (ENA8A-KDV41/Leist): Mains -> "Mains infeed" -> Supply module (Link circuit power 12.5 - 30 kVA) -> "Total continuous drive power 10 - 24 kW" -> Machine; Machine -> "Continuous regen. power > 1 kW" -> Supply module -> "Regen. power" -> Mains.

**2.1 Typical applications**
- *Lathes with modular main drive.* With short cycle times there are high continuous regen. power levels because of the main drives' large load masses.
- *Machining centres with modular main drive.* With short tool-change or machining cycles the high energy inherent in fast rotating main spindles leads to high values of continuous regen. power.
- *Electronically-coupled gear-cutting machines.* During a power failure the master and slave drives can be stopped under the control of the NC controller. High values of continuous regen. power occur during short machining cycles.
- *Other machines and systems with high levels of continuous regen. power.* These can for example be hoists which frequently have to lower large loads over several metres.

## PDF page 12 (printed 10): 2.4 Features

- *Long operating time for connected drives in braking mode.* The energy released when the drives are braked is fed back into the mains with little loss.
- *Supply voltage to power circuits may be varied over a wide range.* 3 x 380 to 460 V, 50/60 Hz power supply possible. No power transformer is required for most applications.
- *The way the drive system reacts to a mains failure can be programmed by means of an external link.*
  - without the link the drives brake at maximum torque
  - with the link a message is sent to the NC controller via a potential-free contact and the drives can be stopped under the control of the NC controller. This protects expensive tools or workpieces from damage.
- *Controlled link circuit voltage.* No reduction in drive response in the event of mains undervoltage.
- *Charging current limiting for link circuit capacitors.* The inrush current can be ignored when selecting the switchgear for the power supply. The service life of the switchgear is increased.
- *Control voltage has high loading capacity.* Up to 10 drive modules can be connected to one power supply module.
- *Earth fault monitor for associated drives.* Power is immediately switched off when the Bb1 contact opens and a visual indication is provided via LEDs in the event of a fault.
- *Ease of servicing.* The electronics power supply and the signal line connections are made via screw terminals.

## PDF page 13 (printed 11): 3. Control circuits

The control circuits refer to power supply ON/OFF switching and the EMERGENCY-STOP circuit. The control circuits suggested by Indramat show the principle of operation. Five control circuits are suggested in this section. The choice of control circuit and its effect on the system depends on the range of functions and sequence of actions of the whole plant and is the responsibility of the equipment manufacturer.

**3.1 Control circuit features**

*A: Immediate or delayed power disconnection during EMERGENCY-STOP.* Even with the power disconnected, Indramat a.c. drives can still be braked. Generally in the EMERGENCY-STOP situation the power can be disconnected immediately. The energy stored in the drive movement is converted into heat by the supply module's bleeder resistor. Stopping the drive must have priority over the overload protection of the bleeder resistor. Frequent, successive and arbitrary emergency-stops (e.g. during commissioning) can overheat the bleeder resistor. Regenerated energy of 100 kWs is allowed within a 5-minute period. If, due to the application, more energy than this is expected to be generated, the power disconnection must be delayed.

*B: Stopping the drives if drive electronics fail, with or without link circuit short-circuit.* In the event of malfunction in the drive electronics the link circuit voltage is shorted as an additional safety measure to brake the drives to standstill. With link circuit short-circuit MAC motors are always braked to standstill, whether or not the drive electronics is still operational. Asynchronous drives are not braked when the link circuit voltage is short-circuited. Without link circuit short-circuit MAC motors slow down uncontrolled if the drive electronics malfunctions. The link circuit short-circuit can only be dispensed with if power disconnection (limit switches overrun), or a drive fault message are sufficient to protect the system from damage.

*C: Controlled braking of drives during EMERGENCY STOP or mains failure, with set-point zeroing by the drive electronics or with position control by the NC controller.* With an emergency stop or mains failure, the drives are usually stopped at max. torque under the control of an internal set-point zero command. In the case of an emergency stop or if the drives' internal monitors are activated, a zero set-point is initiated by the drive controller and the drives are braked under control at maximum torque. In a few applications (e.g. electronically-coupled gear-cutting machines) the drives have to be stopped under the control of the NC controller in the event of a mains failure. In the case of an emergency stop, or if the internal monitors of the KDV 4.1 are activated, drives are braked under position control by the NC controller.

**Fig. 3: Summary of different control circuit features** (tree):

- Drives stopped if drive electronics malfunction -> "With link circuit short-circuit" / "Without link circuit short-circuit"

## PDF page 14 (printed 12): 3.2 Control circuit with link circuit short-circuit for immediate power disconnection for Emergency-Stop

*Application:* In normal circumstances use this type of control circuit. High security is obtained with this type of control for low cost. The monitors built into the drive system are utilized most effectively. Typical application: if the KDV 4.1 is supplying feed drives only; if asynchronous main and feed drives are run from one KDV 4.1.

*Features:* Power disconnection is simultaneous with emergency-stop operation. The energy in the drives is converted into heat. The bleeder resistor in the KDV 4.1 can absorb a max. of 100 kWs in 5 minutes. Stopping the drive must have priority over the overload protection of the bleeder resistor. Frequent, successive and arbitrary emergency-stops (e.g. during commissioning) can damage the KDV 4.1 and are not permitted. With link circuit short-circuit MAC motors are always braked to standstill, whether or not the drive electronics is still operational. The link circuit short-circuit comes into operation only during drive malfunctions. If the Emergency-Stop button is pressed, the asynchronous main drives are also braked. With an emergency stop or when the KDV's monitors are activated (e.g. mains failure) the drives are braked at maximum torque under the control of the drive electronics.

*Mode of operation:* The NCB link (X10/13 - X10/14) **must not be in circuit**. When the emergency-stop button is pressed the mains contactor drops out immediately. The drives' controller enabling signals are disconnected via an auxiliary contact of the mains contactor. This results in internal set-point zeroing of all connected drives, which then undergo controlled braking. A drive fault message via the KDV 4.1 (Bb1 contact), a fault message via the NC controller (servo fault), or overrunning the limit switches results in the mains contactor being de-energized and the link circuit short-circuit then comes into operation.

## PDF page 16 (printed 14): 3.3 Control circuit with link circuit short-circuit for delayed power disconnection for Emergency-Stop

*Application:* In exceptional cases when large amounts of energy (main drives) are converted in heat during braking and there is a risk of thermal overload to the bleeder resistor. The bleeder resistor can be loaded at 100 kWs max. for 5 minutes. Typical application: when asynchronous main drives and feed drives are operated from one KDV 4.1.

*Features:* When the Emergency-Stop is activated the drives are braked first. Power disconnection is delayed. The drive energy is fed back into the mains. The drive energy is only converted into heat in the event of mains failure or drive malfunction. With link circuit short-circuit MAC motors are always braked to standstill, whether or not the drive electronics is still operational. The link circuit short-circuit comes into operation only during drive malfunctions. If the Emergency-Stop button is pressed, the asynchronous main drives are also braked. With an emergency stop or when the KDV's monitors are activated (e.g. mains failure) the drives are braked at maximum torque under the control of the drive electronics.

*Mode of operation:* The NCB link (X10/13 - X10/14) must not be in circuit. During an Emergency-Stop the drives' controller enabling signals are disconnected immediately. This results in internal set-point zeroing of all connected drives, which then undergo controlled braking. Power supply disconnection is delayed by the braking time of the main drive. A drive fault message via the KDV 4.1 (Bb1 contact), a fault message via the NC controller (servo fault), or overrunning the limit switches causes the power to be immediately disconnected. The drives' controller enabling signals are also disconnected by an auxiliary contact on the mains contactor and the link circuit short-circuit then comes into operation.

## PDF page 18 (printed 16): 3.4 Control circuit without link circuit short-circuit for immediate power disconnection for Emergency-Stop

*Application:* In exceptional cases when power disconnection is adequate to protect the plant from damage when overrunning the limit switch or during a drive electronics malfunction. Typical application: when the KDV 4.1 is supplying only asynchronous main drives; when the end positions of the feed axes are adequately damped.

*Features:* Power disconnection is simultaneous with emergency-stop operation. The energy in the drives is converted into heat. The bleeder resistor in the KDV 4.1 can absorb a max. of 100 kWs in 5 minutes. Stopping the drive must have priority over the overload protection of the bleeder resistor. Frequent, successive and arbitrary emergency-stops (e.g. during commissioning) can damage the KDV 4.1 and are not permitted. The link circuit voltage is not short-circuited. In asynchronous drives the link circuit short-circuit has no additional braking effect if the drive electronics malfunction. Asynchronous drives can no longer be braked under control if the link circuit voltage is short-circuited. With an emergency stop or when the KDV's monitors are activated (e.g. mains failure) the drives are braked at maximum torque under the control of the drive electronics.

*Mode of operation:* The NCB link (X10/13 - X10/14) must not be in circuit. When the Emergency-Stop circuit is opened, the power contactor is disconnected immediately. The drives' controller enabling signals are disconnected via an auxiliary contact of the power contactor. This results in internal set-point zeroing of all connected drives, which then undergo controlled braking.

*Note!* With a malfunction in the drive electronics, the drives slow down out of control. This control circuit must therefore only be used if the linear axes of the plant can run up to their fixed mechanical stop without damage if the drive malfunctions.

## PDF page 20 (printed 18): 3.5 Control circuit without link circuit short-circuit for delayed power disconnection for emergency-stop

*Application:* Delayed power disconnection is required if there is a risk of thermal overload to the bleeder resistor by large amounts of energy. The bleeder resistor can be loaded at 100 kWs maximum for 5 minutes. The control circuit can exclude the link circuit short-circuit if power disconnection is adequate to protect the plant from damage when overrunning the limit switches or in the event of a malfunction in the drive electronics. Typical application: when the KDV 4.1 is supplying only asynchronous drives; when the end positions of the feed axes are adequately damped.

*Features:* When emergency-stop is activated, the drives are braked first. Power disconnection is delayed. The drive energy is fed back into the mains. The drive energy is only converted into heat in the event of mains failure or drive malfunction. The link circuit voltage is not short-circuited. In asynchronous drives the link circuit short-circuit has no additional braking effect if the drive electronics malfunction. Asynchronous drives can no longer be braked under control if the link circuit voltage is short-circuited. With an emergency stop or when the KDV's monitors are activated (e.g. mains failure) the drives are braked at maximum torque under the control of the drive electronics.

A drive fault message via the KDV 4.1 (Bb1 contact), a fault message via the NC controller (servo fault), or overrunning the limit switches causes the power and the drives' controller enabling signals to be immediately disconnected.

*Note:* With a malfunction in the drive electronics, the drives slow down out of control. This control circuit must therefore only be used if the linear axes of the plant can run up to their fixed mechanical stop without damage if the drive malfunctions.

## PDF page 22 (printed 20): 3.6 Control circuit for emergency-stop and mains failure with braking of drives under position control

*Application:* In exceptional cases! Mainly in drives which are coupled via the NC controller as electronic gearboxes, and which must not exhibit any angular error during a mains failure or emergency-stop.

*Features:* Power disconnection is simultaneous with emergency-stop operation. The energy in the drives is converted into heat. The bleeder resistor in the KDV 4.1 can absorb a max. of 100 kWs in 5 minutes. Stopping the drive must have priority over the overload protection of the bleeder resistor. Frequent, successive and arbitrary emergency-stops (e.g. during commissioning) can damage the KDV 4.1 and are not permitted. The link circuit voltage is not short-circuited, so energy is available for stopping the drives under position control. The energy stored in the link circuit, or the energy fed back, must be greater than the energy required for exciting asynchronous drives or for return motion. With an emergency stop or when the KDV's monitors are activated (e.g. mains failure) the drives are braked under position control by the NC controller.

*Mode of operation:* The NCB link (X10/13 - X10/14) **must be in circuit**. The power contactor is disconnected immediately the emergency-stop circuit is opened. The NC controller brakes the drives under position control. The drives' controller enabling signals must not be disconnected by the power contactor.

*Note:* With the NCB link closed, the drive modules' set-point zeroing is suppressed if the power supply is faulty. The master controller must always ensure that the drives are stopped, i.e. the master controller must always evaluate the UD contact of the KDV 4 and stop the drives when the contact opens. Otherwise, the drives can be expected to slow down uncontrolled if the power supply malfunctions.

The required transformer rating is calculated according to the following formula:

S = P_DC x √2 x U_sec / 320 V

where S = transformer rating in kVA (mains input); P_DC = link circuit power in kW; U_sec = nominal rating of transformer secondary (voltage at power connection of KDV 4.1).

In the case of direct connection to the mains, the connected load of the drive system is calculated from the above formula. The mains voltage should then be used instead of the transformer's secondary voltage.

## For the retrofit

What this part tells us (only the KDV 4 supply module; no drive-module, NC interface or machine-specific wiring is in this part):

- **Supply module:** Indramat **KDV 4.1-30-3**, regenerative (line-commutated feedback) supply, 3 x 380-460 V ±10 %, 50/60 Hz, link circuit **320 V DC ±10 %** ("300 V d.c. busbars"), 14/22/30 kW continuous depending on extra capacitor (none / CZ02 2 mF / TCM1.1-04 4 mF), chokes GLD14/15 (link) and KD19/20 (commutation). Fan unit LE4, 220 V AC aux supply (500 VA electronics, 1000 VA fans). It also supplies all drive electronics via the 16-way **X1 bus** (±15 VM, +24 VL, UD, Bb, UESS). Any Indramat drive module (TDM/DDS/RAC etc.) kept in the machine needs the KDV (or an equivalent Indramat supply) for both link power and electronics power, so the KDV stays if the drives stay.
- **Status contacts usable by LinuxCNC (potential-free, X10):** Bb1 ready (X10/6-7), TVW temperature pre-warning (X10/9-10), UD power infeed OK (X10/11-12), NH aux voltage (X10/8). Wire Bb1 and UD to inputs (via 24 V interface relays) and into the E-stop / amp-fault chain; TVW as a warning input.
- **E-stop chain (Indramat recommendation):** Bb1 -> K3 "Bb1 decoupling" relay in series with the NC "servo fault" contact; K3 in the mains-contactor (K1) chain together with limit switches S2, E-stop S1, door interlocks S3, OFF S4, ON S5. The drives' enable **RF** is broken by a K1 auxiliary contact (or K8 E-stop relay), which makes the drives brake at max. torque by internal set-point zeroing. Optional DC-link short-circuit contactor K2 + resistor RK (for MAC servo motors) with the 0.5 s re-close lockout. Which of Figs. 4-8 Gildemeister used must be checked in the machine schematics; for LinuxCNC the NC "servo fault" contact (NC) and the RF enable become LinuxCNC outputs (e.g. `motion.amp-enable` / E-stop latch), and NCB X10/13-14 should stay **open** (no position-controlled braking by the NC), since LinuxCNC cannot reliably do braking under position control after a mains loss.
- **Holding brakes:** feed-axis brake Y1 released by K4 = RF and drive Bb; **speed set-point only 100 ms after controller enable ON** (brake release delay). Keep this timing in the LinuxCNC enable sequence.
- **Command interface / feedback / spindle:** not described in this part (the KDV has no command interface). The drive modules (analog ±10 V set-point vs. SERCOS, resolver/encoder feedback, spindle drive interface) must be taken from part b or the drive-module chapters. The manual edition (1991) and the "TVM/TDM and KDV/KDS" bus mention suggest the analog-interface generation of Indramat drives [uncertain until the drive modules are documented].
- **For replacing the drives:** if the Indramat drives are replaced, the KDV 4.1, X1 bus, chokes, C1/CZ02/TCM capacitors and K2/RK short-circuit circuit can all go. The replacement needs its own regenerative or braking-resistor DC supply (the lathe main spindle has high continuous regen. power; Indramat lists >1 kW continuous, 100 kWs per 5 min bleeder limit), and the mains contactor/E-stop logic (K1, K3, K8, K7 delayed drop-out) must be rebuilt around the new drives' ready/STO signals. The T2 transformer branches (220 V servo motor fans via Q4, 3 x 380 V spindle motor fans via Q5) remain needed for the motor fans regardless.
