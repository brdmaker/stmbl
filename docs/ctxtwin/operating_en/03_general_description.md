## 3.1 Concept of the CTX twin

(Page 3-3)

The machine and its controls are designed for front and rear machining, whereby

- two identical or differing work sequences can be performed in parallel or with offset times
- complete machining, i.e. front and rear can be performed within one sequence.

Program functions can be converted automatically, so that the user need only consider a conventional single slide machine.

### 3.1.1 Modular groups

The controls receive 2 modular groups in accordance with the machine concept, termed **AG** in the following.

- each AG is autonomous as far as its operating and programming surface is concerned
- an AG can include several spindles and several slides
- each AG deals with its own working space
- only one operating mode exists at the same time in an AG
- differing operating modes can simultaneously exist in both AG, i.e. operating mode splitting
- with full utilisation of operating mode splitting it is necessary that the working spaces be divided by a separating wall for safety reasons.

## 3.3 Optional assembly groups

(Pages 3-6 and 3-7)

The following assembly groups do not form part of the basic machine execution.

They are available as options.

1. **Bar guide / bar feed:**
   The bar guide accurately guides the bars outside of the headstock spindle.
   The bar guide automatically pushes the bars forward.
   A bar loading magazine also loads bars from a magazine (supply).
2. **Spindle reduction tube:**
   For bar machining tubes are necessary in the turning spindle matched to the bar diameter.
3. **Pick-off device right, including transport belt and part ejector:**
   The pick-off device takes up the finished workpiece from the right hand spindle and feeds it out of the working space on the transport belt.
4. **External data memory unit**
   External data memory unit, e.g. floppy disc Facit N 1100 or GNT 7102 NC data drive
5. **C axis:**
   The use of the C axis permits the manufacture of contours on jacket and front faces in conjunction with driven tools, e.g. pockets, polygons and hole patterns.
6. **Automatic working space separation wall**
   The automatic working space separation wall divides the machine into two working spaces.
7. **Chip conveyor with coolant container**
   The chip conveyor transports the chips out of the working space and simultaneously serves as a coolant container.
   The machine standard execution is equipped with a chip bath, which simultaneously serves as a coolant container.
8. **Oil mist separator**
   The oil mist separator draws oil or coolant mist from the machine working space.
9. **Overload coupling for X and Z axes**
   This option stops the feed in X or Z in the event of an overload.
10. **Tool measuring unit, left and right**
    The tool measuring unit serves for measuring the tool in the machine working space in the MANUAL CONTROL operating mode. This is available on both spindles.
11. **Automatic protection hood, left and right**
    The automatic protection hood option permits an automatic opening and closing of the protection hood, this also applies to the individual working spaces.
12. **Two pressure clamping device, per spindle**
    The two pressure clamping device provides two different programmable chucking forces at the chuck in the machine working spaces.
13. **Two pressure coolant unit**
    The two pressure coolant unit provides two different selectable and programmable coolant pressures in the machine operating space.
14. **Coolant spray gun**
    The coolant spray gun permits the flushing out of chips (soiling) from the working space.
15. **Coolant cleaning**
    The optional coolant cleaning permits the removal of contaminants from the coolant by an additional unit, e.g. belt filter or pressure sieve filter.
16. **Coolant through the turning spindle, per spindle**
    This unit is preferably used for flushing out the clamping means.
17. **Hollow clamping unit 52 dia., left hand spindle**
    The hollow clamping unit permits the machining of bar material of bar sections.
18. **Hollow clamping unit 52 dia., right hand spindle**
    The hollow clamping unit permits the machining of long workpieces in the right hand spindle
19. **Control lamp (3 fold)**
    The control lamp is located on the machine and indicates various operating conditions
20. **Oil and coolant collector bath**
    This bath prevents such substances from penetrating into the hall floor.
21. **Equipment**
    A further adapted range of parts, handling systems etc. are available for the optimum workpiece machining.

## 3.4 Intended use of the machine

(Page 3-8)

**Which parts?**
The Gildemeister lathe is primarily used for turning chucking and shaft components. The basic version permits the machining of parts in accordance with the data for the working space. Refer to 2.4, "Working space page 1" on page 2-6 or 2-5, "Working space page 2" on page 2-7.

**Which materials?**
Using this machine commercially available steel, cast iron, copper, aluminium, brass, gunmetal and machinable plastic as well as artificial resin bond wood can be turned.

If you are not certain whether your material can be turned on the machine, please contact your Gildemeister representative.

**Particular suitability**
The machine is distinguished by its short setting times. For this reason it is particularly suitable for the rational production of small to medium batch sizes at a constant quality.

It permits

- the complete machining within the framework of optimum possibilities
- the parallel machining of identical or similar components on both spindles.

(Note symbol: exclamation mark with hand.)

**Regular cleaning and maintenance**
The machine only operates reliably and without malfunction when it is regularly cleaned and maintained.
For this reason ensure that chapter 8.1 "Maintenance" on page 8-3 is observed.

## 3.5 Warning against misuse

(Page 3-9)

In spite of the safety and stability of the machine some things must be observed:

(Note symbol.) **Do not turn ceramic parts**
Turning ceramic parts creates a fine dust, which enters every corner and quickly impairs important mechanical functions. Furthermore, it can block the lubrication and cooling system.

(Note symbol.) **Observe limit values**
Take into consideration when programming the specified max. rotational speeds of the main drive and chucking means as well as the maximum feed forces, refer to 2.1, "Technical data" on page 2-3. Under no circumstances exceed these values!

Take note of the increase in feed force with the wear of the tool cutting edge.

(Note symbol.) Furthermore, take into consideration the manufacturer's data concerning permissible chucking pressure and force of the chucking means as well as the maximum number of indexings per hour tool holder limit value, refer to 2.1, "Technical data" on page 2-3.

## 3.6 Safety devices

(Page 3-10)

This machine is equipped with the safety devices described in the following. In addition further safety measures form an integral part of the machine and controls.

(Warning triangle.) All safety devices are intended for personal protection. It is forbidden to put these devices out of operation or bridge them.

Ensure that the safety devices are reset to their safe condition following repair work.

Include safety devices in the regular maintenance routine, refer to chapter 8.1, "Maintenance" on page 8-3. Replace parts subject to wear as early as possible.

### 3.6.1 Key switch

Access to certain operating modes or settings is only possible using a key switch or a password. The operator must ensure by suitable operating directives that this key is only accessible to instructed and authorised users.

Set the parameters or setting values in accordance with the operating means employed, e.g. chucks. These must be adapted for each change.

### 3.6.2 Emergency stop cut-out

These buttons with a noticeable red/yellow marking must be actuated in the case of danger.

All movements are stopped. Drives which could imply a danger are isolated.

Reset the emergency stop button after overcoming the dangerous situation and prior to restarting.

### 3.6.3 Agreement button

It is only possible to start dangerous movements in manual mode when the agreement button is pressed with one hand and the function key with the other. The movement is stopped when one of the buttons is released.

### 3.6.4 Cut-out safety devices in the machining space

(Page 3-11)

The machining space represents particular dangers as a result of the movement of slides, tool holders and spindles. It is encapsulated on all sides, access is only possible via a mechanically actuated protection hood(s) linked with the controls. It is only possible to start dangerous movements when the protection hood(s) is (are) closed.

The access conditions are dependent upon the type of movement:

- with the protection hood(s) open
  - run the spindle at max. 50 r.p.m using the function key and the agreement key
  - Clamp and release
  - Swivel the tool holder at reduced speed using the function key and agreement key.
  - Move the chip conveyor free in manual mode using the function keys and agreement keys
  - Move the slide at reduced speed, max. 1 m/min. using the function key and the agreement key.
- Movement of options for setting as long as the relevant function and agreement keys are pressed.
- All movements are possible with the protection hood(s) closed.

Dimensioning of panelling and the protection hood(s) with their safety windows is such that any parts flying out as a result of breakage or collision will be securely held back. This only applies where permissible rotational speed and the chuck jaw weights are not exceeded.

Ensure that damaged parts are exchanged following a collision, this applies in particular to the safety window and its mounting.

#### 3.6.4.1 Automatic working space separation wall (operating mode splitting)

In order to practically use the possibilities of operating mode splitting an "Automatic working space separation wall" option is available. This ensures that with the individual use of both working spaces (various work sequences parallel or time offset), no danger to the operator results as when closed it is not possible to reach into the other working space.

### 3.6.5 Speed control

(Page 3-12)

The spindle rotational speed (0 r.p.m, 50 r.p.m and the max. value) are individually monitored. It is only possible to open the protection hood when the spindle is stationary, the spindle only starts up when the protection hood is closed.

Refer to the technical data for the max. permissible machine speed.

Enter the value as a parameter when the max. chuck speed is lower than the maximum rotational speed of the machine. In addition, this value must be set on the separate rotational speed limitation for power chucks (option). Access to these settings is only possible via key switch or password.

### 3.6.6 Workpiece clamping

Workpiece clamping safety is ensured with the following means:

- Foot switches generating the chucking movement, however, stop the movement when released.
- Chucking pressure monitoring, i.e. insufficient clamping pressure prevents a start up or cause a stoppage of the turning spindle.
- Clamping stroke movement, i.e. when the chucking jaws are in one of the controlled end positions, they prevent the turning spindle from starting up.
- Safety clamping cylinders which maintain the chucking force in the event of a line breakage until the turning spindle has stopped.

Reset the proximity switches for the chucking stroke monitoring and the chucking pressure when changing chucks. Change the limit speed as a parameter and in the speed monitoring.

Check and record at regular intervals (see maintenance plan) the clamping forces on the power chucks, in accordance with information given in the chucking force diagram using a commercially available clamping force gauge. This also applies after changing the chuck.

### 3.6.7 Hydraulic group

(Page 3-13)

The pressure in the hydraulic system is monitored, the machine is stopped if the set value is gone below.

### 3.6.8 Pneumatic (when installed)

The pressure in the system is monitored in the pneumatic system, the machine is stopped when the set value is gone below.

### 3.6.9 Cut-out devices for setting and maintenance spaces

The machine is panelled on all sides.

During setting and maintenance work, in which dangerous movement are performed, access is only permitted through doors or flaps which are electrically controlled and with special tools.
A plate on these doors and flaps normally indicates that they may only be opened when the machine is stationary, otherwise danger of a tool breakage exists.

### 3.6.10 Noise protection

The machine is designed such that the noise emission values at the workplace stated in the technical data sheet are not exceeded.

Cutting noises could exceed the specified value under unfavourable conditions. In such a case reduce the noise emission values by changing the cutting conditions, tools or clamping conditions.

### 3.6.11 Emissions

The machine is prepared for connection to an oil mist extraction system.

### 3.6.12 Chip conveyor

(Page 3-14)

Chip conveyor operation is only permissible when the chip bath is correctly positioned below so that it is not possible to reach into the ejection shaft of the chip conveyor.
Do not remove chips manually.
The machine must be switched off to clean the chip ejection opening.

- 5% glycol as rust preventative

### 3.7.5 Handling lubricants and coolants lubricants

(Page 3-18; heading as printed.)

Warning (triangle): **Oil, lubricant and coolant lubricants**
Due to their additives, these products imply a possible danger to health and environment.

Selection and application are exclusively in the hands of the owner. The operating instructions to be prepared by the owner covering the handling of these materials should contain:

- Material designation
- Danger to persons and the environment
- Protection measures and behaviour, e.g.
  - Wear protective gloves made of resistant plastic
  - Avoid contact with the skin and eyes
  - Do not inhale fumes and vapours
  - Do not use fire, naked flame and smoke
- Behaviour in the event of danger, first aid
- Correct disposal

Please clarify in time how the used lubricants and coolants can be disposed of in an environmentally sound manner.

During the turning cycle lubricant fumes might develop. Gildemeister offers an oil mist separator for their disposal.

## For the retrofit

- Control type: not named in this chapter. The control is organised as 2 "modular groups" (AG), one per side, each autonomous in operation and programming, each with its own working space and one operating mode at a time; the two AG may run different operating modes simultaneously ("operating mode splitting") (p. 3-3). This maps directly to one LinuxCNC instance per side.
- Programs can be "converted automatically" so the user programs a conventional single slide machine; front and rear machining in parallel, time-offset, or as one complete sequence (p. 3-3).
- Axes: X1, Z1 (left, X1/Z1 compound slide), X2 (X2 slide), Z2 (right: Z2 feed motor moves the right spindle side, see figure); integrated (motor) spindles left and right; optional C axis with driven tools (pp. 3-4, 3-5, 3-6).
- Tool holders: one per side (disk turrets in figure); driven tools via a servo motor on the tool holder; max. indexings per hour limit in chapter 2.1 (pp. 3-4, 3-5, 3-9).
- Chucking: hydraulic clamping cylinder + tension tube + chuck/collet per spindle; foot switch per side (movement only while pressed); chucking pressure monitoring and clamping-stroke end-position proximity switches inhibit spindle start / stop the spindle; safety cylinders hold force on line break; optional two-pressure clamping (programmable) and 52 mm hollow clamping; chuck speed limit is a parameter, separately set on optional power-chuck speed limiter (pp. 3-5, 3-7, 3-12).
- Interlocks the control must implement: protection hood(s) only openable with spindle stopped; spindle starts only with hood closed; spindle speed monitored at 0 rpm, 50 rpm and max.; with hood open only agreement (enable) key + function key moves: spindle max. 50 rpm, turret swivel at reduced speed, slides max. 1 m/min, chip conveyor jog, clamp/release; key switch or password for certain modes/settings; E-stop isolates hazardous drives (pp. 3-10 to 3-12).
- Hydraulic pressure and pneumatic pressure (if fitted) monitored; machine stops below set value (p. 3-13).
- Options with I/O implications: automatic separation wall between the two working spaces (needed for operating mode splitting), automatic protection hoods left/right, right-side pick-off device with transport belt and part ejector, bar feed, tool measuring unit left/right (MANUAL CONTROL mode), overload coupling X/Z stops feed, two-pressure coolant, coolant through spindle, 3-fold control lamp, chip conveyor, oil mist separator (pp. 3-6, 3-7, 3-11).
- Part transfer: the chapter only says complete front+rear machining is possible in one sequence; no M-codes or signals given here (p. 3-3).
- Supply cabinet holds switch cabinet, hydraulic unit, lubrication unit and the spindle-motor cooling unit (water + 5% glycol) (pp. 3-5, 3-17).
- Fluids: hydraulic oil min. 100 l (HLP32), lube oil container 3 l, automatic lubrication approx. 0.5 l per 100 operating hours; coolant 310 l; chuck greased every 8 operating hours (p. 3-15).
