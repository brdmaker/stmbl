### 9.2.5 Mains requirements (p. 168, per IEC 204-1)

- Frequency fluctuation ±2 %; voltage fluctuation (no-load and full load) +6/-10 %; mains interruption at U_N max. 10 ms.
- Other mains voltage than 3 x 400 V: use a transformer.

### 9.2.6 Voltage stabilizer

- Same text as 6.9.3: use a regulator if voltage cannot be guaranteed; weak networks may lose more voltage than a regulator compensates. Machine uses regenerative braking (energy fed back to mains on axis/spindle deceleration), which can destroy an unsuitable stabilizer; ask Mikron AG Nidau After Sales.

### 9.2.7 Pneumatic supply requirement

- Required pressure 6 bar; consumption with oil/air lubricated spindle max. 300 l/min.
- Caution: compressed air quality must meet ISO 8573-1 class 5/4/5, otherwise the machine, especially the incremental length measuring systems (scales), may be damaged.

## 10. Appendix (p. 172-173)

### 10.1 Water-miscible coolants (emulsions)

- Minimum: excellent corrosion protection, high resistance to micro-organisms, does not attack paint, no sticky residues, high emulsion stability, gentle to skin. Selection per VKIS data sheet.

| Property | Unit | Test method | Standard value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concentration | % | Refractometer | 4 % - 12 % |
| pH value | | DIN 51369 | 8.5 - 9.4 |
| Foreign oil | % | Refractometer | < 0.5 % |
| Thermal conductivity | kcal/mh°C | | > 0.45 |
| Sticky residues | | VKIS test sheet 521-01 (DIN E 53538/3) | without sticky residues |

- Note: also follow the coolant manufacturer's specification.
- Caution: check concentration weekly with a hand refractometer; if too high (evaporation), top up with thin mix.
- Warning: check odour and pH weekly; bad smell or acid pH means microbial infection: replace coolant and clean the whole coolant system.

### 10.2 Cutting oils

- Machines are designed for water-based emulsions, not cutting oils; check compatibility with the coolant system first.
- Caution: higher viscosity of cutting oils can damage the coolant system or cause malfunctions.
- Warning: inhaling oil mist/vapour is harmful; see chapter 5.2.4.1.
- Caution: cutting oils can form flammable aerosols, ignited by friction or tool breakage (worn tool, programming error); for unattended operation install monitoring that immediately shuts the machine down.

## For the retrofit

- Control is iTNC 530 (rating plate, manual list and declaration), not iTNC 520. Declaration serial 107.87.00.096 (Feb 2005) is likely this machine's number [uncertain; verify on the cabinet rating plate].
- Original drives: Siemens Simodrive 611 D (documentation on request from Mikron).
- Axis naming in the manual: tilting axis is B (swivel, ±110°), rotary is C; check whether the machine actually tilts about X (A) or Y (B) before setting up LinuxCNC kinematics.
- Axis performance targets: X/Y/Z 0-40 m/min, 1 g (10 m/s²), AC servo 3000 rpm via toothed belt to ballscrew (ratio/pitch needed from elsewhere); B 150 rpm (54'000 °/min), 100 rad/s²; C 250 rpm (90'000 °/min), 300 rad/s².
- Travels HSM 400U: X 400 (-200/+200 about table centre), Y 240 (-160/+80 about table centre), Z 350, B ±110°; max workpiece Ø230 x 350 mm, 25 kg on the rotary table. These help seed soft limits and the pivot/table-centre reference.
- Spindle (kept): Step-Tec HVC 140, 30'000 / 42'000 / 60'000 rpm variants, min 100 rpm, oil-air lubricated (needs air, max 300 l/min at 6 bar); tool limits for the U: ATC outer circle Ø65 x 155 mm, 2 kg (1.5 kg HSK32).
- Electrical: 3 x 400 V, 16 kW installed, 12.5 kW nominal, 80 A peak, 30 AT line fuse, 6 mm² leads, 24 V DC control voltage, mains interruption tolerance 10 ms, regenerative braking to the mains on the original drives.
- Air quality ISO 8573-1 class 5/4/5 protects the scales (incremental length measuring systems, sealing air implied); keep the scale purge air when retrofitting.
- Tool changer 18/36/68 places (HSK E 40) or 20/40/80 (HSK E 32), 10 s tool change, 12.5 s chip-to-chip; WPC 7 or 20 pallets, 90 s change, 90 kg.
- Cooling unit 5.8 kW, 5 l/min (spindle/motor cooling circuit, control must switch/monitor it).
