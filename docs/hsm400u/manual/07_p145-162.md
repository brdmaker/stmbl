## 6.5 Interim storage (p. 146)

- If not installed soon after delivery: store protected, vibration-free, dust- and humidity-free, same ambient conditions as for operation (see 9.2.2).
- Keep the machine on its wooden pallet; never rest it directly on the ground.
- For long-term storage add conservation; the delivered rust preventive works only for a limited time.
- Check for transport damage before storage; transport damage generally must be registered within 7 days of receipt (observe the forwarder's claim deadline).
- Note: if storage is outsourced, clarify liability.

## 6.6 Scope of supply (p. 146)

- Check completeness against order confirmation / delivery note; report transit damage or missing parts to Mikron immediately in writing.

## 6.7 Ambient climate (p. 146-147)

- Thermal behaviour decisively affects accuracy (deviation desired/actual) and precision (repeatability).
- Constant, well-known ambient conditions are crucial for high accuracy.
- For high accuracy demands, install in an air-conditioned room.
- Distance to heat radiators, other machines, walls/ceilings, windows: at least 1.5 m sideways and 2 m in height.
- Avoid direct sun through windows; prevent large temperature changes from opening doors/windows.
- Fig.: plan view showing permitted blowing directions of the cooling unit's exhaust air (two arrows away from the machine, from the cooling unit at the rear right).
- Note: do not expose the machine to warm/cold air streams from blowers or draft.

### 6.8.1 Requirements on the foundation and ambient conditions

- Have a structural engineer check floor load-bearing capacity (load per unit area, force distribution in concrete).
- Site: free from vibrations; no local/unilateral heating or cooling (radiators, sun, drafts); free from HF electrical interference; concrete or end-grained wood recommended.
- Note: composite floors (concrete and end-grained wood) are not permitted.
- Floor unevenness max. 4 mm/m; floor must be horizontally even in the area.

### 6.8.2 Lifting square

- Lifting square (yellow) attaches at four positions Pos. 1-4 shown in the plan sketch.
- Warning: lift the machine only with the lifting square; no hydraulic jacks etc. in direct contact with the polymer concrete column (risk of fracture of the machine base).
- Warning: lift with the lifting square before the WPC is attached.

## 6.8.3 Assembly of the vibration absorber (p. 149)

- Vibration absorbers isolate machine from building; fitted before final erection.
- With the machine raised, insert the 4 level pads at positions 1-4 per drawing (1, 2 at rear, 3 under the front/WPC side, 4 front left) [positions per sketch].

## 6.8.4 Levelling the machine (p. 149)

1. Put a water level on the work table (longitudinal and cross).
2. Turn the hex socket on level pads 1, 2 and 3 until level within 0.05 mm/m.
3. Lock with the counter-nuts. Re-levelling may be needed later on uneven/weak floors.
4. Pad 4 is only an additional support: place a dial gauge near pad 4 against the bottom edge of the base, screw pad 4 up until the base lifts by 0.02 mm, then fix the counter-nut.
- Note: if the optional chip removal unit is supplied, adapt the machine installation height to it.

## 6.8.5 Transport safety (p. 150)

- Caution: remove the yellow transport locks of linear axes X and Y before commissioning, otherwise machine and drive systems may be damaged.
- Pos. A, Y-axis: loosen the six screws on the transportation bar (1), remove the bar.
- Pos. B, X-axis: loosen the four screws on the transport safety device (2), remove it, then screw in and tighten the four original telescope-cover screws.
- Note: keep the transport locks; refit them for any further transport. Drive the axes to the correct position before switching off so the locks can be fitted.
- Caution: lifting/transport without the locks can cause uncontrolled axis movements and damage.

## 6.9 Wiring up the machine (p. 151)

- Observe local regulations (cable cross-sections, fuses); connection only by skilled electricians or Mikron service.
- Procedure:
  1. Retighten all screw terminals on terminal strips, contactors, relays and fuses in the cabinet; check plugs are seated.
  2. Check mains requirements per section 2.3.4.
  3. Main switch to "0"; open cabinet door.
  4. Route supply cables through the screwed ducts on the machine roof to the supply terminal.
  5. Switch mains ON and check rotary field. Caution: right-hand (clockwise) rotary field required.
- Fig.: supply terminal X0 with terminals PE, L1, L2, L3, N, PE.
- Warning: connect earth with care; incorrect connection endangers persons and property. High voltage.

### 6.9.1 Adaptation to mains fluctuations (p. 152)

- Rated voltage 3 x 400 V / 50 Hz. Guaranteed operating range 3 x 360 V to 3 x 425 V / 50 Hz (EN 60204-1).
- 60 Hz mains needs special adaptation on the machine side (cooling appliance, extraction system).
- Other 3-phase systems need voltage adaptation (see 6.9.2).

### 6.9.3 Voltage stabilizer

- Recommended if the operating voltage (see 9.2.5) cannot always be guaranteed.
- Weak supply networks may have extra losses from undervoltage/higher currents that a regulator cannot compensate; get detailed mains data.
- The machine uses regenerative braking: energy is fed back to the mains during axis and spindle decelerations, which can destroy an unsuitable stabilizer. Choose per actual conditions; ask Mikron AG Nidau After Sales.

## 8. Options (p. 154-162)

### 8.1 Chip removal unit (CRU) (p. 154)

- Choice: chip basin (manual chip removal, with or without coolant-emulsion equipment) or CRU (automatic). With CRU, coolant emulsion equipment and manual spray gun are always included.
- CRU = longitudinal conveyor (1) under the work area + cross conveyor (2); chips and coolant fall into (1), which carries them to the lower cross conveyor; (2) lifts chips up an ascending stage into a chip container or trolley.
- Warning: never reach into or step into a running CRU.

### 8.2 Touch probe m & h PS 32.00 mini (PR) (p. 155)

- Two probe types: m & h PS 32.00 mini, Renishaw MP 700. Mikron recommends the m & h PS 32.00 mini (size suits the traverse range).
- MP 700 working: locates/measures/digitizes workpieces. Loaded into the spindle like a tool, manually via button S461 or by the tool changer.
- Spindle orientation (cycle 13 and M19) stops the spindle at the same angle so the probe always works in the same orientation (repeatability).
- Infrared data receiver B422 sends a switch-on signal every approx. 1-2 s; battery-powered probe switches on when it enters the transmission field.
- When B422 registers "switched ON" from the probe, any spindle start (M3 or M4) is locked.
- When the probe leaves the field (back in magazine or out of work area), the receiver switches OFF after approx. 1 min.
- Maintenance note: with a flat battery the receiver gets no "presence" signal; message "SCANNING SYSTEM NOT READY".
- More info: HEIDENHAIN User's Manual "TOUCH PROBE CYCLES", chapter 4, and probe manufacturer manuals.

### 8.4 Minimal quantity lubrication unit (MLU) (p. 157-159)

- Unilube oil-mist unit nebulizes oil for tool-edge cooling; switched ON/OFF with M07 [only M07 named; OFF function not given].
- Parts: 1 tank with fine filter 0.3 l; 2 adjusting knob nozzle 1; 3 tube to nozzle 1 (transparent); 4 adjusting knob nozzle 2; 5 tube to nozzle 2 (transparent); 6 bleeding/drain screw; 7 adjusting turning knob; 8 screw cap 0.3 l; 9 electrically controlled valve with manual override; 10 compressed air connection; 11 time-lag relay with control valve; 12 adjusting knob pump 1; 13 adjusting knob pump 2; 14 dosing pump (1 per nozzle).
- Two adjustable nozzles (6 in photo) on the spindle slide (Step-Tec spindle) aim at the cutting edge.
- Note: spray only a minimal quantity; mist forms in the work area, extraction system recommended.
- Maintenance/adjustment: see manufacturer's manual.
- Lubricants: Coolube 2032 E, Mikron item 928.09.00.001 (tough material, alloy steel, non-ferrous); Unilube 9107, Mikron item 928.09.00.051 (aluminium, light and non-ferrous alloys).
- Pneumatic diagram (German labels "Minimalschmiereinheit Unilube", "Düse 1/2"): 6 bar supply -> 3/2-way solenoid valve (ports 1, 2, 3; pilot 12, spring return 10) -> dosing pumps fed from the tank -> two check valves -> nozzle 1 and nozzle 2.

### 8.5 Oil mist extraction unit (OMEU) (p. 160-161)

- Electrostatic filter on the protection cabin; ionizes particles, which settle on charged or grounded aluminium plates; clean, dry air exits laterally.
- Switched by M38 = ON, M37 = OFF.
- Maintenance: clean metallic pre-filters and electric filter cells regularly, at least every 6 months (more often with sticky oil).
- Warning: before maintenance switch off and lock the main switch; the unit stays at dangerous voltage approx. 10 s after switch-off, do not open earlier; risk of death. See chapter 5.1.
- Parts: 1 switch ON/OFF; 2 failure pilot lamp; 3 fine fuse; 4 speed controller; 5 air outlet; 6 mains connection.
- Cleaning procedure:
  1. Switch machine OFF (Q111) and stop the filter with switch (1).
  2. Open the maintenance door via rapid-action locks (2); safety switch (5) on the door additionally disconnects the mains [numbering as in text].
  3. Pull out metallic pre-filter (8) and electric filter cells (7).
  4. Soak in grease-solving cleaner (per manufacturer); replace soiled filter mats.
  5. Let drip dry; reassemble in reverse order.
- Caution: no aggressive/corrosive agents, no pointed or sharp tools.
- Caution: operate only with pre-filter and filter cells installed.
- Cleaning set (container + agent): Mikron item 928.75.20.010.
- Failures: unit not working = fine fuse (3) blown, switch (1) off, no M function programmed, power interrupted. Failure lamp (2) on = filter cells heavily soiled, HF contact surfaces soiled/defective, ionization wires broken/defective.
- Technical data: filter performance 1100 m³/h; clean air outlet lateral/horizontal; electric filter cell surface 2 x 6.3 m².

### 8.6 Dust extraction unit (DEU) (p. 162)

- Stand-alone graphite dust extractor beside the machine, via extraction frame; connected to the machine by a plug.
- M40 dust extractor ON, M39 OFF; M42 filter cleaning ON, M41 OFF.
- Maintenance and technical data: supplier's manual.

### 8.7 Tool cleaning by air blast (p. 162)

- Air blast nozzle on the laser tool-measuring device (Blum) blows on the tool; for graphite dust, oil droplets, small chips.
- Activated automatically with each measurement.
- Fig.: photo of the nozzle on the laser unit.

## For the retrofit

- Regenerative braking: the original drives feed energy back to the mains on axis/spindle braking (6.9.3). If the original spindle drive (Simodrive 611 D per p. 163) and its infeed are kept, the regen path stays; stmbl axes need their own braking resistor/DC-bus handling.
- Mains: 3 x 400 V 50 Hz, operating 360-425 V, right-hand rotary field required, supply terminal X0 (PE L1 L2 L3 N PE). Optional 19 kVA isolating transformer.
- Remove the X and Y transport locks before first motion; refit before transport (axes must be parked in the right position first).
- M-functions the new PLC/HAL must reproduce: M07 MQL on (off code not given), M37/M38 oil mist extraction off/on, M39/M40 dust extractor off/on, M41/M42 filter cleaning off/on, M19 / cycle 13 spindle orientation, M3/M4 spindle start.
- Touch probe interlock: IR receiver B422 polls the probe every 1-2 s; spindle start (M3/M4) is locked while the probe reports ON; receiver times out after ~1 min. Probe load button S461. Message "SCANNING SYSTEM NOT READY" on flat battery.
- Handwheel: mushroom E-STOP in the pendant (must be in the E-stop chain), two enabling buttons (right/left) for jogging with door open, encoder, axis keys X/Y/Z/IV/V with LEDs, release key that must be held together with an axis key, feed override potentiometer.
- MLU valve: 3/2 solenoid on 6 bar air with a time-lag relay; laser air blast fires automatically with each tool measurement.
- OMEU on main switch Q111 circuit; failure lamp is local to the unit (no PLC signal mentioned).
- Optional CRU conveyors (longitudinal + cross) are PLC-switched devices [control signals not given here].
